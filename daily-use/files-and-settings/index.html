    Files & Settings¶

    Your device has a real little filesystem (LittleFS) and crash-safe settings. This page covers looking around, what survives resets and updates, and how to keep a backup. For the command-by-command syntax see Console & Commands; for Tasmota background see Coming from Tasmota.

    Looking around: the filesystem shell¶

    Four commands, identical on every chip, all answering in JSON:

    > DF
{"status":"OK","total":1048576,"used":16384,"free":1032192}

> LS /
{"status":"OK","path":"/","files":[{"name":"autoexec.be","size":128,"type":"file"},{"name":".newmota","type":"dir"}]}

> MV "/old name.txt" "/new name.txt"
{"status":"OK","from":"/old name.txt","to":"/new name.txt"}

> RM /temp.txt
{"status":"OK","removed":"/temp.txt"}
    • df — total, used, and free bytes.
    • ls <path> — list files and folders.
    • mv — rename or move (quote paths with spaces).
    • rm — delete one file or one empty folder. There is deliberately no recursive delete.

    You can also work with the filesystem from your browser: the Web Interface file browser lists, uploads (files, folders, drag-and-drop), edits text files, and deletes (empty folders only). Newmota's internal settings files are protected from browser reads and writes. Berry scripts can use the path module for the same operations.

    Your settings live in two slots, /.newmota/settings.a and /.newmota/settings.b, each with a checksum and generation counter. A change rewrites the slot holding the older copy in one atomic LittleFS commit: a success reply means the new copy is stored, and a power cut mid-save leaves the previous copy in the other slot instead of corrupting your configuration. At start Newmota uses the newest valid copy. If a slot cannot be read, Newmota starts from the other one and refuses setting changes until a Reset, so it never overwrites settings it could not read. Newmota never formats the filesystem on its own; a missing filesystem is reported, not silently replaced. On ESP32, Newmota also includes a bootstrap importer for Tasmota 15.5.0.2 settings layouts.

    Other durable files sit beside them:

    • The boot count, energy totals, sensor policy, Modbus buses and the audio, webcam, Ethernet and deep-sleep settings use the same two-slot scheme, for example /.newmota/boot.a and boot.b: a save rewrites the older slot, and the newest valid one is used at start.
    • /.newmota/matter/ holds Matter fabrics, device identity and CASE sessions. They are stored unencrypted, so treat a flash backup as sensitive. See Matter.
    • /mi32cfg holds BLE sensor devices, keys and names in Tasmota's format; MI32Cfg writes it. See Bluetooth & BLE Sensors.
    • /.newmota/web-identity.bin holds the HTTPS certificate and private key on HTTPS builds and is protected from browser access.

    What survives what¶

    Event Wi-Fi credentials Other settings & files
    Restart / power cut ✅ kept ✅ kept
    Reset 1/2 (factory reset) ❌ cleared ❌ cleared
    Reset 4/5/6 (keep-Wi-Fi reset) ✅ kept ❌ cleared
    Reset 3 (radio recalibration) ✅ kept ✅ kept (only radio calibration is re-done)
    Wired update, most platforms ✅ kept ✅ kept
    Wired update, Beken ✅ kept ⚠️ may be erased — the filesystem overlaps the OTA staging area by design

    On Beken, treat your own files as disposable across updates and keep anything important (Berry scripts, templates) copied on your computer. A Beken factory flash (flash, flash-monitor, flash-only) goes further: it initializes LittleFS and removes Newmota's Wi-Fi credentials from the NET records, so the device starts from fresh Newmota configuration.

    On Bouffalo, LittleFS is part of the layout (64 KiB on BL602 2 MiB, 320 KiB on 4 MiB targets), but the one BL602 hardware trial did not have LittleFS or settings available, so persistent storage there is still untested.

    Backing up¶

    Two layers, for two kinds of disaster:

    1. Your configuration as text — copy the outputs of Template, SSId1, and your SetOption choices somewhere safe. Re-typing them restores your whole setup in minutes.
    2. The full flash image — the backup.bin you took during installation restores the device bit-for-bit, including the stock firmware you replaced. Don't lose that file.

    If settings ever get into a state the firmware doesn't understand (for example after flashing an older build over a newer one), changed-value commands fail safely instead of writing garbage, and the console tells you. An explicit Reset — not a restart — clears the lock by writing fresh defaults.

    SD cards¶

    Optional USE_SDCARD (ESP-IDF, Bouffalo, AmebaD, Pico) routes /sd and every path below it to a FAT volume on an SD card through the vendored FatFs; all other paths stay on LittleFS. Listing / shows sd while the card is mounted, renames stay within one volume, and web uploads stage on their destination volume.

    The card mounts once at boot, right after GPIO init, with Tasmota's pins: SDCard CS with the SPI pins of its bus (instance 2 wins over 1), otherwise SDIO CMD, CLK and D0, four-bit when D1–D3 are all assigned. It is never formatted; the log names why a mount failed (no card, no FAT volume, unusable pins). Files carry local time once the time service is synchronized, 2026-01-01 before. A controlled restart or deep sleep syncs open writers and unmounts the card first. ESP-IDF supports SPI and SDIO cards; Bouffalo, AmebaD and Pico support SPI mode, and BL616/BL618DG also four-bit SDIO; on Beken the card reports unavailable. The ESP32-S3-EYE example uses a one-bit SDIO card.
